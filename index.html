03 / What makes the loop rigorous

A promising gap has to survive three challenges

Contribution

Is the uncertainty important?

A new combination of model, language, population, and apparatus is a starting point. Explain what understanding or decision would change, and why existing findings cannot settle it.

Prior work

Has someone already answered it?

Search alternate terms, adjacent fields, references, citing papers, and preprints. Read the closest work deeply. Record sources, queries, dates, exclusions, and remaining coverage gaps.

Study design

Can we actually learn the answer?

Check competing explanations, measures, apparatus reliability, recruitment, resources, and supervisor fit. State what a positive, null, or contradictory result would teach us.

04 / The outcome

5–10 distinct directions.
A reason to pursue each one.

A shortlist to examine with the student. Each option includes its strongest objection, remaining uncertainty, and the evidence that could invalidate it.

Inside each surviving direction
  • Research question, contribution, and closest competing work.
  • Source-linked motivation and an explicit novelty argument.
  • Linked subquestions and a feasible 3–3.5-year programme, adjusted to the target.
  • First-year deliverables, dependencies, and fallback paths.
  • Programme-specific requirements and a student-developed proposal plan.

Honest conclusion: “No directly answering study found within this documented search scope.” Absence of a search result is not proof of novelty. Reading and critical decisions remain the student’s responsibility.
